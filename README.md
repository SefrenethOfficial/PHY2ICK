# PHY2ICK

The in-between chapter of [Physick](https://github.com/SefrenethOfficial/Physick): after the lockdown, before whatever comes next. Less story-driven than Physick, but the city's on edge, and people talk.

Play it at **https://sefrenethofficial.github.io/PHY2ICK/**

## The island

The map is the island from `PHY2ICK_Map.svg` (1 px of the drawing = 1 m): the same coastline, every road (with sidewalks, kerbs, lane markings and zebra crossings), and every building and place, each with its own look and sign. A **minimap** (top right) turns with the camera.

It's a **dense** city, not an open one: every gap between the places on the map is packed with buildings (around 150 of them, shops at street level, alleys between), the empty north of the map is a **grid of streets lined with blocks**, and cars are parked along the kerbs and in the car parks. Any parked car is yours: walk up and press `F`.

- **The sea all round**, a sea wall where land meets water, and **the inlet** cutting in from the south-west, with a **bridge** where the road crosses it. **Palm Tree Beach** slopes into the water along the south, with palms, umbrellas, towels and a lifeguard tower. **The Pier**: a timber boardwalk all the way up the east coast, with lamps and benches.
- **The west block** (the triangle by the inlet): Game Shop, Home Improvements (×2), Cheap House, **Putin's Ritz** (a hotel with a red carpet), City Wok, **Shitty Wok** and **Triangle and Co.** (triangular buildings), and a fenced vegetable plot. **The sewer entrance** is a big culvert in the inlet's bank, half under water: you have to swim to it.
- **North of the avenue**: the **Hospital** (red crosses, an ambulance bay, a helipad; you wake up here if you black out), the **Skatepark**, the park with the **Playground** (swings, a slide, a climbing frame), the fountain, the **Nightclub** (neon sign, queue rope), **Paul Badfella's law firm** (behind a fence you have to jump), the **Bourke Office High Rise**, the **Coffee Store** with **PRIVATE INVESTIGATOR** on the window upstairs, **Grace's Flowers**, the little park, and the **Police Station** (in its own road loop, police cars outside).
- **Downtown**: A Rival Mini Mart, the Indoor Pool, a row of shops, Mini Mart, Visitor Info, **Town Square** (a stone plaza with a statue, trees, benches and lamps), the Pawn Shop and the Grocery (with an alley between them), the **Diner** (long, red, chrome, a neon sign) and its car park.
- **The south**: the Spa, **Fairview Heights** (where you live), the Convenience Store, the **construction site** (a half-built multi-storey car park you can drive or climb up, a crane, scaffolding, site hoarding), the **abandoned car park** (broken, graffitied, a burnt-out car), **Albuquerque Boiled Turkey** (with a drive-thru window and menu board on the loop road round it), its car park, the hedge, and the beach's washrooms, reception and car park.

### Inside
Walk in through the front door of most places: there's a real room behind it, lit, furnished, with someone working there you can talk to.
- **Shops** (both Mini Marts, the Convenience Store, the Grocery, the Game Shop, both Home Improvements, Cheap House): proper gondola shelving (plinths, end panels, price strips, hanging aisle signs) stocked with rows of products (cereal boxes, cans, bottles, bags, game cases, paint tins, depending on the shop); glass-door **fridges and freezers** along the back with lit shelves of drinks and frozen food; an open **chest freezer** by the window; the till with a scratchies stand, and a back counter behind the shopkeeper (tobacco and lotto, pre-orders, key cutting…). **The Pawn Shop**: glass cases, guitars on the wall, a man who's seen it all.
- **The Diner**: checkerboard floor, booths along the windows, a counter with stools, a jukebox.
- **The Coffee Store**: counter, tables, and a stair up to the **Detective Office** upstairs: a desk, filing cabinets, and a corkboard of photos joined with red string (PRATT, MERIDIAN, DOCKS 3AM, WHO PAID?). The detective is in.
- **The Nightclub**: a dance floor that lights up to the beat, a bar, a DJ booth, booths. **The Indoor Pool**: a deck, lanes, a lifeguard, and water you can swim in.
- **Lobbies** with a front desk: the **Hospital**, the **Police Station** (a wanted board), **Putin's Ritz** (marble, a chandelier, a red carpet), **Paul Badfella's** (bookshelves, carpet), the **Spa**. Restaurants: **City Wok** and **Albuquerque Boiled Turkey**.

### The title screen
The PHY2ICK logo over a timelapse on the beach by Kit's van: the day runs by in seconds (dusk, a short night, dawn). Wren sits on the sand in a yellow hazmat suit; you come out of the back and hand her the bags; she walks off along the beach, sells to the dealers there, and comes back and sits down again. Now and then a police car pulls up by the dealers and a cop gets out to ask a few questions, and they suddenly find their phones, the sea and a nice tune very interesting. The title theme plays over it (once you've clicked or pressed a key: browsers won't play music before that).

### Shops, stealing and selling
- **Shelves:** the Mini Marts, the Grocery, the Game Shop, Home Improvements and Cheap House have real things on the shelves (chips, cans, cereal, games, paint, hammers…). Pick one up, take it to the till and hold `E` on the clerk to **pay**. Or press `V` to **pocket** it and walk out. If the clerk sees you pocket it, or notices you leave, they call the police (crouching helps).
- **Pickpocketing:** crouch, or get behind someone, and hold `E`: *Pickpocket*. Get it right and you lift their cash, and maybe a watch, a phone or a necklace. Get it wrong and they shout, and they'll call it in.
- **The Pawn Shop** buys whatever's in your pockets (stolen things for half what they're worth, things you paid for for less). It also sells sacks and Blue Salts, and buys Physick.
- **Halyard Arms**, the gun shop downtown (where the visitor kiosk was), sells the pistol, the shotgun, ammo and a hatchet.

### Rusty's Parking
A small parking lot on the east side, $5 an hour, says the sign. At the back, a garage marked *STAFF ONLY*. Drive a car in, stop in the bay and press `E`: Rusty will **register it as yours** ($300), **respray** it ($150, ten colours) or put **new plates** on it ($100). Every car has its own plates now, and the police **remember cars**: use one in a crime, or let them see it in a chase, and it's a *known car* (it shows under your weapon). Drive past a cop in a known car, even with no stars, and they'll recognise it. A respray or new plates makes it a different car. If the police are after you and nobody saw you drive in, Rusty pulls the shutter down and they lose you.

### The story
PHY2ICK is a prequel to PHYSICK: the time before Wren is gone. Press **Play** and it starts at the beginning.

You wake up in an ice bath in the shared bathroom at the end of your corridor, in your boxers, with no memory of the night. Before you open your eyes you choose who you are: skin tone, hair (what shows under the beanie) and hair colour. Then: the cold, the panic, *why am I in ice?* Your clothes are on your bed in 1E, in a room that's had a party in it. Channel 6 is on, talking about a blue substance. Your phone has last night on it.

Wren's on the stairs, on the phone to the hospital. Her younger brother **Kit** put you in that bath. Then he lay down on your floor and didn't get up. She needs a lift to the hospital.

At the hospital she goes in alone. An hour later she comes out, and you put a hand on her shoulder. Then **Vic** turns up: their older brother, in a red convertible and sunglasses, back from a "retreat", too busy *investing in himself* to help with the funeral. Wren walks off. Vic asks you to help her, however you can, and tells you about **Kit's van**: an old camper parked up on the beach. He hands you Kit's keys. *"And whatever he was doing in that thing… don't."*

In the van: Kit's hoodie, a burnt pan, a notebook that says **MERIDIAN**. Kit was making it. He made what you two took. His phone won't stop buzzing with people who want more, the funeral needs paying for, and a man called **Pratt** has left four voicemails about what Kit owed him. The deal: **you cook, Wren sells**, and Vic never finds out. There's enough under the sink for one batch. Wren waits outside, leaning on the van, talking to you through the wall. Give her the bags and she moves in with you (a mattress on your floor in 1E) and sells them to Kit's buyers, a bag at a time, while you're out. Bring her more at home, or take her along (hold `E` on her).

### Wren
Once she's moved in, Wren is your partner, not a passer-by: she never calls the police on you, doesn't run off or put her hands up, and can't be mugged, grabbed or put in a boot. She reacts in her own words instead: to fights, guns, stolen cars, chases, frisks, your driving, your cooking.
- **Trust** (Strained, Guarded, Okay, Close, Family) goes up when you talk to her, hang out, take the bags to her yourself, answer her texts and cook clean batches; it drops when you commit crimes in front of her, get her chased, hurt her, ignore her or snap at her. Closer, she says more (Kit, Vic, Pratt), texts you first, and gets better prices from her buyers.
- **Hold `E` on her**: *Talk* (how she's holding up, Kit, Vic, the business, Pratt, just chat), *Hang out*, *Give her the bags*, *Come with me* / *Head home*, *Thank her* / *Say sorry*, *Snap at her*.
- **Your phone (`P`)**, after the first cook: text her to *come and get* the bags (she turns up where you are), or say you'll *bring them to her* (she tells you where she is: home, the pier, outside the nightclub, the beach by the van). Quick texts, a call, and her texts to you: sales, worries, Pratt, Vic. Leave her on read too long and she notices.
- **After the third cook** she changes: louder, faster, prouder, and she calls you **Chef** (*"Yeah, Chef! Blue magic!"*). She has **ideas**, and you can say yes or no: a night at the nightclub, a buyer at the construction site who pays double, bigger batches, higher prices, *KIT'S KITCHEN* painted on the van, riding along with you for the day. Each one has a catch. Her mood changes through the day (buzzing, flat, wrecked) and with it how she talks and how fast she sells.

It isn't a run of missions: a few scenes, a line in the corner telling you what's going on, and the rest is the city. (Kit left debts. Pratt wants them paid. Vic, their older brother, is too busy with his midlife crisis to help. That's all still to come.) If the car blows up with Wren in it (or she dies) before she's moved in, that part starts again from the last checkpoint: the stairs, the hospital, or the van. (Once she's living with you, she's patched up at the hospital and back home a minute later.) People who follow you follow the way you went: through doors, down stairs, round corners, and they catch up if they get stuck. `Enter`, `Space` or `Escape` skips a scene.

### Home: Fairview Heights
Your room is **1E**, on the first floor of your building at **Fairview Heights**, on the south avenue. It's not big: a single bed under the window (which looks out over the street), a desk with a little TV showing Channel 6, a wardrobe, a mini fridge, a couple of boxes you haven't unpacked. Out the door is a corridor with four more flats, and a stair down to the lobby and out the front door. Your car's parked outside.

- **The neighbours.** Each flat is furnished for whoever lives there, and each of them can become a friend. Hold `E` on them to talk, **hang out** (once a minute or so), give them $20 or pay them a compliment (now and then, not over and over). They go from *Stranger* to *Neighbour* to *Friend* to *Close friend*, greet you when you pass, and once you're friends they'll do you a **favour** (every few minutes):
  - **Mrs. Okafor**, 1A, a retired teacher: **dinner**, which heals you fully.
  - **Dez**, 1B, a musician who keeps weird hours: lends you **$150**.
  - **Marco**, 1C, a mechanic who never sees nothing: lets you **lie low** at his place. A report, or up to three stars, and the police lose interest.
  - **June**, 1D, who knows everyone's business (and has a cat): **gossip**. She tells you which Physick buyers are undercover cops.
  Insult them and they'll go off you.

- **Pick things up.** A mug, books, cans, a pillow, the boxes, a basketball, and a traffic cone (don't ask). `Click` to pick something up and carry it, `Click` again to put it down, `Right-click` to throw it.
- **The weapon rack** ("IN CASE OF EMERGENCY") on your wall: a **Hatchet**, a **Butter Knife** and a **Plastic Kiddie Knife**. Press `E` at the rack to take one (or put it back), `Click` to swing, `X` to put it away. The hatchet drops people; the butter knife makes them bleed and run; the plastic knife bends, and people laugh at you.

### Moving about
- **Swimming**: go into the water and you swim (front crawl when moving, treading water when still). `Space` at a low edge pulls you out.
- **Vaulting**: `Space` in front of something low (a fence, a wall, a bench) and you vault it.
- **The walk**: a long, loose stride: the heel lands on a nearly straight knee, the knee folds through the swing, the foot rolls off the toes; the hips sway and turn, the shoulders turn against them, the arms swing a little across the body. Running leans in, drives the knees and pumps the arms.
- **Carrying**: small things (a mug, a bottle) in your right hand; big things against your chest in both hands.

### People
- **Built like the first game's people, with more parts.** Blocks for the pelvis, belly and chest, upper arms, forearms, hands (with a thumb), thighs, shins and shoes on soles, with round shoulders, elbows and knees between them; short or long sleeves, a belt. A round head with **just eyes and eyebrows**: no nose, no ears, no mouth.
- **Faces that change.** The eyes and brows have their own little bones: people look happy when you give them money or compliment them, angry when they come after you, annoyed or sad when you insult them, surprised when a car nearly hits them, scared when they run, screw their eyes up when they're knocked down, and close them for good when they die. Everyone blinks, and their brows move when they talk.
- **You** wear a dark knitted beanie.
- **Proper hair**: short, side-parted, swept, buzz, curly, a big afro, long, a bob, a bun, a ponytail, or bald.
- **Animated from a skeleton.** Every person has a joint rig (hips, spine, chest, neck, head, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles) and a gait driven by speed: a walk blends into a run, arms swing against the legs, the body bobs and leans, knees bend on the swing. They breathe when standing, gesture when they talk, turn their heads to look at you, sit on benches, point when giving directions, and sit properly in cars with their hands on the wheel. Each person is a single mesh (one draw call).
- **Citizens** keep to the sidewalks, walking along beside the road; at a corner they turn or cross. Before crossing they stop at the kerb, **look left, right and left again**, and wait until nothing's coming. Now and then they cross over mid-block, stop for a moment, sit on a bench, or **go shopping**: in through the door, along an aisle to look at the shelves, to the till to pay (the shopkeeper says something back), and out again. They step round each other and jump out of the way of cars coming at them. Each has a name, a **mood** and a **memory** of you: how often you've talked, given them money, complimented or insulted them, or run them over.

### The interaction wheel
Walk up to someone and **hold `E`**. Time slows down and a wheel opens with their name (once you've met) and how they feel about you:

- **Talk:** they introduce themselves, then gossip (the Hollows bombing, the army at the naval base, a ship with no flag, Pratt out on bail…), or small talk. They remember you ("You again!", "It's the twenty-dollar guy!").
- **Follow me / Stop following:** if they like you enough, they come along. Get in a car and they'll get in the passenger seat; get out and they get out too.
- **Give $20:** makes their day.
- **Directions:** they point the way and set a waypoint (a beam of light and a marker on screen) to somewhere on the island.
- **Compliment** and **Insult:** insult someone enough and they'll either run off or come over and shove you.

Move the mouse toward an option and let go of `E`, or click, or press `1`–`6`. `Q` cancels.

### Cars
- **Real vehicle physics** (Rapier's raycast vehicle): each wheel has its own spring and damper, so the car squats when you accelerate, dives when you brake and leans in corners. The tyres grip and then slide; the handbrake (`Space`) locks the rear wheels for handbrake turns. Rear-wheel drive, power fades toward top speed, steering lock eases off at speed, and the brakes are front-biased so it stops straight.
- **A real cabin.** Press `C` for the inside view: the dashboard with working speedometer and rev counter (and a gear indicator), a radio screen, a steering wheel that turns with the front wheels, seats, door cards, and a **working rear-view mirror**. Your character's hands are on the wheel and follow it round. Mouse to look around inside.
- Doors, handles, side mirrors, headlights, tail lights that brighten when you brake, reversing lights, number plates.
- Cars hit people: they get knocked down, then (depending on how brave they are) come after you or run. Drive at you and you'll be knocked flat too.
- `R` puts a flipped car back on its wheels.
- **Get out at any speed.** Press `F` while moving and you throw yourself out: you tumble along the road (it hurts more the faster you were going) and the car rolls on without you.
- **Cars float.** Drive off the sea wall and the car bobs on the water; you can still (slowly) motor about and steer like a boat. The bridge over the inlet is solid to drive and walk on.
- **Parked cars** line the kerbs and fill the car parks: press `F` by one to get in. About half are **locked**: you put your elbow through the driver's window (glass everywhere) and climb in.
- **Doors open and close**: getting in, the driver's door swings open, you climb in and sit down, and it shuts behind you; getting out, it opens again. Cops' doors, passengers' too.
- **The boot**: hold someone (`G`) and press `F` at the back of a car to bundle them into the boot (it opens and slams). `G` at the boot throws in someone lying on the ground nearby (hurt or dead), or lets out whoever's in there. They thump and shout to be let out.
- **A fatal crash** leaves you slumped over the wheel, the horn blaring.

### Crime and the police
- **Witnesses, not mind-reading.** The police only come after you when they know it was you. A cop who sees a crime comes straight for you. Otherwise **witnesses** (anyone nearby who's facing you, with a clear view) pull out their phones and call it in a few seconds later: hit them or scare them off before they finish the call. Some crimes always get reported: a robbery (the till has an alarm), a fire. Crouching makes you much harder to spot.
- **Reported.** A call makes you a **suspect**: hollow, blinking stars and *REPORTED* under them. A police car comes to the scene quietly, no siren, and the cops get out and search the area. If a cop sees you while you're reported, they recognise you from the description and the real chase starts. Stay out of sight and the report goes cold.
- **Patrols.** A few cops walk the streets. Walk past with a weapon out and they'll tell you to put it away (then come for you if you don't); point it at one and they come straight for you. Now and then (more if you run about near them) a cop **stops and searches you**: stand still with your hands up for a few seconds. If they find a hatchet, a gun or bags of Physick, you're under arrest. Run off mid-search and they chase you anyway.
- **Wanted stars (five).** Once they're after you, crimes earn stars: hitting someone or firing a gun (one), dealing or carrying drugs (one), killing someone, robbing a shop or kidnapping (two), attacking a cop (three), killing one (four), and doing it again while they're after you adds more.
- **The police come for you**: one black-and-white **police car** per star (each arriving from a different direction), lights flashing and siren wailing, finding its way to you along the roads. Near you, each car pulls up at its own spot round you (no pile-ups: they slow as they arrive, and steer round other cars in the way), and two cops jump out and spread out round you, each keeping their own distance. At **one star** they try to arrest you; from **two** they shoot. In a car the nearest two chase and ram you while the rest follow behind; drive off and the cops get back in theirs.
- **Lose them**: get out of sight, then out of the area. For the first few seconds after the call goes out they know where you are. Once they lose sight of you the stars blink and the area they're searching shows on the minimap, flashing red and blue (bigger with more stars). Get out of it and stay out of sight and they give up (about 40 seconds at two stars, longer with more). Hiding inside it works too, but much more slowly. Cop cars and cops on foot show on the minimap.
- **The chase theme** plays while you're wanted (*Chase on Foot*, the one-to-two-star theme from PHYSICK), fading in and out.
- **Give up** with `Y`: hands up, and they come and cuff you.
- **BUSTED / WASTED**: slow motion, the world goes grey, the words come up. Busted, you're released at the Police Station with a fine (they keep your guns). Wasted, you wake up at the Hospital with a bill.

### Fighting
- **Fists**: click to punch (jab, jab, hook: the third can knock them down). Hold right mouse for a guard. Brave people put their fists up and fight back; the rest run.
- **Guns**: a **pistol** and a **pump shotgun**, from the Pawn Shop (ammo too), or picked up where a cop fell. Hold right mouse to aim over the shoulder, click to fire, `R` to reload. Head shots count triple. Bullets hit people, cars (they smoke, burn and blow up) and loose things.
- Switch weapons with `1`–`6` or the mouse wheel: fists, the kiddie knife, the butter knife, the hatchet, the pistol, the shotgun (the ones you've got).
- **Robberies**: aim at a shopkeeper with anything from a **butter knife** up (not your fists, not the plastic knife) and they empty the till while you keep it on them. Aim at anyone else and they put their hands up and drop their wallet.
- **Kidnapping**: `G` grabs someone in front of you: a **sack** over their head if you've bought one (shops sell them), a hand over their mouth if not. They struggle along with you; `F` by a car bundles them into the passenger seat, where they stay. `G` lets them go.
- **Finishing people off**: someone badly hurt lies on the ground moaning for help. `Q` near them: a stamp, a stab, or a shot.

### Physick (code name: Meridian)
The drug the game is named after. It's made up, and the cook is a game, not chemistry.
- **Supplies**: *Industrial Solvent* from the Home Improvements clerk ($30) and *Blue Salts* from the Pawn Shop ($45). One of each makes a batch.
- **The cook**: hold `Space` to raise the heat and keep the needle in the green zone (which drifts about). When it flashes **STIR!**, hit `E`. Too hot for too long and it **boils over and blows up**. `Q` gives up (the supplies are wasted). The better you keep it in the green, the bluer it gets: **purity** is your score, and it sets the price.
- **Where to cook**: **Kit's camper**, parked up on the beach (you get the keys in the story). Go in the back door and there's a whole room in there: a little stove, a sink, a bench bed, the notebook, the curtains drawn. Cook at the stove: 8 bags a batch, a little smoke out of the vent. Drive it anywhere you like. Boil over in there and the van goes up. If Wren's with you, she waits outside, leaning on the van.
- **Selling**: buyers hang about the **skatepark**, **the park**, the **beach washrooms** and outside the **nightclub** (the best price, but the bouncer takes 20%). Talk to them (`E`) and sell one or five. Or dump the lot on the **Pawn Shop** for a low price. Some buyers are **undercover cops** (June can tell you which). Dealing in one spot too often gets you reported. Your bags show under your weapon.

### Blood
- Wounds soak into people's clothes and skin where they're shot, stabbed or hit by a car (a head shot, a red head); exit wounds spray out behind. Explosions leave people scorched. Pools spread under bodies.

### Crashes and explosions
- **Crashes** dent the body where you hit, throw sparks, and knock the car's health down. A badly damaged car smokes; one that's had enough (or is left upside down) catches fire and **explodes**, throwing the wreck into the air and knocking down (or killing) anyone near. The burnt-out wreck smoulders.
- Hit something hard enough head-on and you go **through the windscreen**.

## Performance

One graphics setting, tuned for speed: no post-processing, a 2048 shadow map that follows you, and the resolution eases down by itself if frames run slow (and back up when there's room). People off screen or far away aren't drawn or animated; far-off people think and move a quarter as often; parked cars are drawn in neighbourhood batches of three meshes each, so whole streets of them are skipped when out of view.

## Running it

```
npm install
npm run dev     # http://localhost:5173
npm run build   # the built game goes in dist/
```

Pushing to `main` builds and deploys to GitHub Pages (`.github/workflows`).
